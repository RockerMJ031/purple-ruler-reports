How to read the score (0–10)

Score = raw / 180 × 10. Each peak slot contributes days × factor(days) where factor follows a convex curve {1:0.3, 2:0.6, 3:1.0, 4:1.8, 5:3.0}. The curve heavily rewards being present across all 5 weekdays. Counter-intuitive consequence: missing one weekday drops a perfect score from 10.0 to 4.8, while cutting peak hours in half (9–3 → 9–12) only drops it from 10.0 to 5.0. 5-day × half-peak ranks above 4-day × full-peak, because allocator flexibility is more valuable than total declared hours.

Recommended management bands:
■ ≥ 7.0 — "Established" / high-commitment tier (premium allocation, retention priority)
■ 5.0–7.0 — "main workforce" (the productive middle, cover most demand)
■ 3.0–5.0 — "part-time / limited" (allocatable but constrained; watch hours-per-week)
■ < 3.0 — "marginal — review" (likely not viable for peak demand)
Caveat: this score reflects declared availability, not delivered hours. A teacher with score 10 who only delivers 3 classes/wk in Import86 is wasted supply. True "good productivity" = high score AND high actual Import86 hours.

4. Per-slot supply score

Formula: score = depthNorm × (0.7 + 0.6 × reliableShare) × 10, where depthNorm = weightedSupply / (teacherCount × 5 × 3.0) and weightedSupply = Σ heads(d) × d × DAY_FACTOR[d] for d ∈ {1..5}.
Range [0, 13]; "10" = nominal full health (all teachers covering 5 days), >10 = exceptional. Bar color encodes reliableShare (5-day teachers / any-day teachers); the right-hand annotation shows reliable / vulnerable head counts (1-2 day teachers).
How to read the annotation on the right of each bar:
• reliable XX% — reliableShare (5-day teachers / any-day teachers). Drives bar color and the multiplier.
• Y/Z heads — reliableHeads / anyHeads. Z is total teachers offering this slot; Y is the 5-day subset. The gap Z − Y is the fragile pool.
• vuln N — the 1–2 day teachers inside that gap. HR conversion target — moving any of them to 5-day lifts the slot directly.

Quick judgment — three independent signals:
Signal Healthy Watch Critical
Score (overall) ≥ 7.5 6.7 – 7.5 < 6.7
reliable% (color) ≥ 85% 78 – 85% < 78%
vuln (HR target) < 5 5 – 10 > 10
Use all three together — score and color are independent signals (score weights depth × reliability; color is reliability-only). A slot can be "watch score, healthy color" (thin but reliable, e.g. 13:30 = 6.85 / 81%) or "watch score, critical color" (mid depth, fragile mix, e.g. 14:30 = 6.87 / 74%). Best slots win all three columns; worst slots lose all three.

Worked examples:
• 09:00 = 7.58 / 88% / vuln 5 — healthy across all three. Promise it.
• 13:30 = 6.85 / 81% / vuln 9 — watch. Depth is mid but reliability is decent — safe for short bookings, not for SEND.
• 14:00 = 6.59 / 74% / vuln 11 — critical on all three. Either pair with a backup or move the booking.

Mid-afternoon slots (13:00, 14:00) have the lowest scores not because depth collapses, but because reliableShare drops to 73–76%, dragging the multiplier from ~1.23 down to ~1.14.

5. What this means for each team

The same chart, read through five different lenses. Each team optimises a different decision — the score and its components surface different signals depending on who's reading.

Operations & Scheduling routing decisions

Watches: bar color (reliableShare) and slot ordering.

  • Green slots (≥85% reliable): safe to promise long-running placements, single-teacher contracts, and SEND/exam classes where churn is expensive.
  • Red slots (<78% reliable): treat as fragile. Pair with backup teachers, avoid stacking high-risk students, and flag for ops review before confirming.
  • Action: when building a weekly grid, route confirmable demand to green slots first; only spill into red slots after a contingency teacher is identified.

Recruiting & HR hiring targets

Watches: right-hand annotation — reliable / vulnerable head counts per slot.

  • Vulnerable count (1–2 day teachers): these are the conversion candidates. Moving a vuln teacher to 5-day status lifts the slot's multiplier far more than hiring a brand-new 1-day teacher.
  • Slot-specific gaps: e.g. 14:00 sits at 74% reliable with 11 vulnerable heads — converting 5 of them lifts the slot from 6.59 to ~7.4 without any net new hires.
  • Action: prioritise contract-upgrade conversations over external recruiting for any slot already at >100 anyHeads but <85% reliable.

Account Management & Sales commercial framing

Watches: score ranking and the gap between green and red slots.

  • Time-of-day pricing: green slots (09:00–10:30) are the premium inventory; red slots (13:00, 14:00) carry delivery risk and should not be sold at the same SLA.
  • Customer conversations: when a school requests an unstable slot, surface the score — "this half-hour has 26% non-5-day coverage" is a defensible reason to negotiate a different time.
  • Action: bake the score into the booking-confirmation flow so AMs see the slot's health before committing capacity.

Finance cost & risk

Watches: reliableShare trend across the week (proxy for substitution cost).

  • 1–2 day teachers carry higher per-hour effective cost: more onboarding overhead, more no-show risk, more last-minute substitution payments. A red slot's hidden cost is not the headline rate.
  • Multiplier drag: a 10-point drop in reliableShare reduces the slot's effective capacity by ~6%, which translates directly into either lost revenue or substitution spend.
  • Action: when modelling unit economics, use depth × reliability rather than raw headcount to size the deliverable supply curve.

Product & Data model calibration

Watches: distribution of scores and whether the 7 / 4 thresholds still match operational reality.

  • Calibration check: if a slot scored "red" but ops never reports incidents, the threshold may be too pessimistic. If a "green" slot routinely fails, the 5-day weight (DAY_FACTOR[5]=3.0) may be under-rewarding stability.
  • Demand fusion: this metric is supply-only. The next step is to overlay demandShape per slot — high-demand red slots are the highest-priority incident risk; low-demand red slots are nuisance, not crisis.
  • Action: revisit DAY_FACTOR and the reliability base/range (currently 0.7 / 0.6) quarterly against actual confirm-to-deliver rates.

Leadership portfolio view

Watches: the overall shape — is the green band wide enough for the business plan?

  • If 6+ of 12 slots are red: the supply pool is structurally fragile, not just under-staffed in spots. Strategy is to convert teacher contracts, not hire harder.
  • If green slots concentrate at the start of the day: growth is bounded by mid-afternoon capacity. Capacity expansion (or pricing pressure) should target 12:00–14:30 specifically.
  • Action: read this chart monthly alongside churn and headcount; treat reliableShare like a leading indicator for delivery quality.

6. North Star — the slot score is the temperature

The Chart 4 score is the North Star — a single [0, 13] "temperature" reading that compresses depth (how many teachers × their 5-day commitment) and stability (% who commit 5 days) into one number. 10 = nominal full health. Three aggregation lenses (per-slot, 12-slot average, % above threshold) are different views of the same metric, not competing candidates.
What the temperature actually means — decomposing 10:30 = 7.7

7.7 means this slot's supply health is at 77% of nominal full. It is not "how many teachers are available" — it compresses depth and stability into one comparable number.

10:30 decomposition Value Meaning
Weighted supply 1,579 / cap 2,490 depthNorm = 0.634 63% of theoretical depth — 118 of 166 teachers offer this slot, weighted by 5-day commitment
86% of those teachers are 5-day multiplier = 1.21 Reliability bonus, range [0.7, 1.3]; 86% earns a 1.21× multiplier
Anchor "healthy" at 10 × 10 Calibration so slots are comparable; >10 = exceptional, < 7 = under-supplied
= 7.7 77% of nominal full health

Why [0, 13] not a percentage? The cap leaves headroom above "healthy" for exceptional slots (multiplier can reach 1.3 when 100% of teachers are 5-day). Anchoring at 10 makes "this slot is below health" or "this slot is exceptional" visually intuitive without capping the metric at 100%.

Sensitivity — how much a single HR action moves the temperature

Per slot the action affects (using 10:30 = 7.7 as baseline):

Action Δ temperature Note
1 teacher: 4-day → 5-day at this slot +0.07 Moderate lift; ~4 such conversions to add +0.3
1 teacher: 1–2 day (vuln) → 5-day +0.10 Highest per-action ROI — ~3 conversions to add +0.3
New 5-day teacher covering this slot +0.02 Smaller per-slot — depth normalises to teacher count
Lose 1 5-day teacher at this slot −0.08 Same magnitude as a 4→5 promotion gain
1 teacher: 5-day → 1–2 day −0.11 Worst single event — symmetric to vuln → 5d

HR insight: converting an existing 3–4 day teacher to 5-day (+0.07 to +0.09) is roughly 3–4× the per-slot impact of hiring a new 5-day teacher (+0.02) — cheaper, faster, and lifts the metric immediately. The metric is intentionally slow-moving: it takes sustained action, not single heroics, to shift a slot from yellow to green. That is what makes it a credible North Star.
